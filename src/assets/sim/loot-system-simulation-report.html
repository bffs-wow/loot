The Reality of WoW Loot: Not Everyone Gets Everything

In real World of Warcraft raiding, gear is not uniformly distributed. Actual guild wishlist data reveals 18 raiders competing for a single S-Tier Trinket (e.g., Purified Bindings of Immerseus) that drops only ~4 times in an entire 16-week phase. Over 70% of raiders will never receive it. Below is the true test of each loot system: Who wins the ultra-scarce items, who finishes their 4-piece tier set, and who gets left behind?

Hoarder Benefit — Measured per System (300 seasons each)

1. Standard EPGP — the refusal lever is the whole game

Minors cost GP (400 each). Lurkin wins 5.5x the cohort-average S-tier rate (98.3% vs 17.9%, edge +80.4pp). He refuses armor, keeps GP at the floor, and wins contested majors by priority. Final GP: Lurkin 1040 vs cohort 1335 — GP gap is the cause, not a side effect.

2. Free minors — the lever disappears

Minors at 0 GP: everyone's GP path through armor is identical, so refusal saves nothing. Lurkin 23.3% vs Goatlord 27.3% (cohort 22.1%, edge +1.2pp). The refusal advantage disappears.

3. Additive boost — no PR exists, so no hoard

Roll systems have no Priority Rating — outcome is decided by /roll + attendance boost, so nothing to refuse and nothing to hoard. Lurkin 25.3% vs cohort 20.3% (+5.0pp). No GP lever, no edge.

4. Armor at MS price — the trap inverts

With only ~50% of majors desirable, Lurkin lands below cohort: 16.0% vs 22.1% (-6.1pp). He is barred at MS from junk, then wins OS junk at 25% price on highest PR — paying GP for items his refusal was meant to avoid. Meanwhile Bexy (greedy) claims junk at MS and still wins 78.3% of S-tier drops in this sample — priced systems punish refusal and reward indiscriminate claiming alike.
